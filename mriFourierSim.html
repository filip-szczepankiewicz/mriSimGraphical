Readout chord

Each voxel sounds as ρ·cos(2πf t + φ) at the carrier frequency f. The sum of them is one tone with amplitude |S(k)| and phase ∠S(k), so moving in k-space changes its loudness. A steady phase is not audible; a jump in phase is heard only as a brief transient.

Play readout switches on a read gradient along x: each column of voxels precesses at its own frequency, fc + n·Δf, so the coil hears a chord. As time runs, kx sweeps across the line you point at, the arrows turn faster towards the edges, and the chord swells into an echo at kx = 0. Pointing at another ky line changes the starting phases (phase encoding), not the notes. The readout repeats; one sweep lasts 1/Δf.

Δφ is the phase difference between neighbouring voxels. 180° per voxel is the edge of k-space (the Nyquist limit). The spot share is the projection of its sum s(k) onto S(k), Re(s·S*)/|S|²; it is negative when the spot opposes the total.

Fourier sim

After a gradient with area such that k = γ̄∫G dt, the transverse magnetization in a voxel at position r carries the phase φ = −2πk·r. The receive coil adds the magnetization of all voxels, so the signal at that k is the vector (complex) sum S(k) = Σ ρ(r)·e−i2πk·r. Point at k-space to see the phase pattern this produces across the object and how the vectors add or cancel.

Simplifications: the object is a grid of voxels, and each voxel is one vector at its centre with a length equal to its mean spin density (the partial-volume average over the voxel). There is no dephasing inside a voxel, so S(k) is exactly the discrete Fourier transform of the voxel image, and the sampled k-space is periodic. No relaxation, no off-resonance, no noise, uniform coil sensitivity, and the object is real (no phase of its own). Point objects occupy one voxel at the nearest grid position, so their spacing can shift slightly when the matrix changes. Phase is shown with a cyclic colour scale (red = 0). The order of the tip-to-tail chain is row by row and does not affect the sum. The sound puts the sum back on an audible carrier, Re S·cos(2πf t) + Im S·sin(2πf t) = Re{S*·ei2πft}, with S(0) at full volume; in a scanner the same sum is received at the Larmor frequency and demodulated. The sign is chosen so that the faster clockwise precession at +x (higher field for γ > 0) sounds as a higher pitch. In readout mode kx(t) sweeps −N/2 to N/2 in time 1/Δf, so the column at index n from the centre sounds at fc + n·Δf; the waveform is computed sample by sample from S(kx(t), ky). The audio clock is exact; the picture follows it at the screen's frame rate. The readout loops seamlessly because the sampled k-space is periodic, and the carrier is rounded to a multiple of Δf for that reason. Amplitude and phase changes are smoothed over about 20 ms to avoid clicks.

The MR brain is one axial slice of the ICBM 2009a nonlinear symmetric T1-weighted template (MNI152), an average of 152 adult scans, skull-stripped and downsampled to 128 × 128, shown in radiological orientation. Being an average, it is smoother than a single scan. Its grey values are used as spin density, so it stands in for a real image rather than a true proton-density map.
