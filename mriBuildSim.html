    More phase steps keep the FOV (the table grows), so the voxel along y shrinks; a larger table amplitude at the same steps shrinks both FOV and voxel. The ADC keeps its duration, so a shorter dwell gives more samples over a larger FOV at the same voxel size along x; to change it, lengthen the ADC or change Gx.

    What to look for

    An RF pulse played during a gradient excites only a slab, at the position where the Larmor frequency matches the pulse frequency; its thickness is the pulse bandwidth over the gradient. The second half of the slice gradient leaves spins dephased through the slice, so a rewinder is needed. A prephaser moves k-space to its edge so the readout crosses k = 0 mid-way (the gradient echo). A refocusing pulse inverts the phase accumulated before it, which forms a spin echo, and also reverses the sign of earlier gradient areas. Three pulses also store magnetisation along z and give a stimulated echo.

    Simulating…

    Build sim

    A teaching aid, not a quantitative simulator. Students assemble a sequence from a few building blocks — RF pulses, trapezoidal gradients on three axes, a phase-encoding table and ADC events — and see which slab each pulse acts on and the image the sequence produces. The point is which components are needed and why (slice selection, rewinders, prephasers, phase encoding, refocusing), not realistic timing.

    Signal model. Each RF pulse is an instantaneous rotation at its centre, acting with its full flip angle inside its slab and not at all outside it (ideal rectangular profile). The slab lies where the RF frequency equals the Larmor offset from the gradient at the pulse centre, f = γ̄ G·r; its thickness is the bandwidth (time–bandwidth product 4) divided by γ̄|G|. Between pulses the magnetisation is followed as coherence pathways (the extended phase graph idea): each pathway carries a gradient moment k = γ̄∫G dt and a dephasing time τ, and each pulse splits it into transverse (+, −) and longitudinal parts. All pathways are added, so spin, stimulated and gradient echoes, and their unwanted cousins, appear by themselves. A pathway that is still in the transverse plane at readout contributes the 3D Fourier transform of the object it came from, evaluated at its own k at every ADC sample, so an unrewound slice gradient dephases the signal through the slab and a wrong prephaser moves the echo.

    Simplifications. Ideal rectangular slice profiles, no off-resonance during RF, no chemical shift, no diffusion, no B₀ map: field inhomogeneity is a Lorentzian line (T2′), so a pathway is weighted by e−|τ|/T2′. T2 decay is taken at the centre of each ADC event. The sequence is repeated once per phase-encoding line with ideal spoiling at the end of every TR, and the longitudinal magnetisation is in its steady state. The matrix and FOV follow from the sequence (dwell time, read gradient, number of phase steps and the table's amplitude); readout is along x and phase encoding along y. The phantom (from Seqret) is sampled on a 128 × 128 grid in-plane and integrated through the slab.
