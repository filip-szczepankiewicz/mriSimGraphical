What to look for

One idea, used twice. A gradient makes the resonance frequency depend on position. Choosing a band of frequencies therefore chooses a range of positions: the RF bandwidth picks the slice, the receiver bandwidth spans the field of view.

Slice. Double Gs and the slice halves. Change Gs without moving f0 and an off-centre slice moves too, unless the centre frequency follows.

Readout. The N samples split the receiver band into N bins of BW/N; each bin is one voxel. A stronger Gr at the same BW shrinks the FOV and the voxels.

Fat. Fat precesses about 434 Hz below water at 3 T, but the reconstruction places every frequency as if it were water. Low pixel bandwidth gives a large shift: bright and dark bands appear where fat meets water along the readout, but not along y.

Cost. More bandwidth at the same FOV means a stronger Gr and a shorter readout: less fat shift, less SNR. Thinner slices and smaller voxels also cost SNR.

Aliasing. Tissue outside the FOV has frequencies outside the band and folds back into the image.

Bandwidth sim

A gradient G makes the resonance frequency depend on position, f = γ̄G·x (in the rotating frame, γ̄ = 42.58 MHz/T for ¹H). Selecting a band of frequencies therefore selects a range of positions. In slice selection the band is set by the RF pulse, so Δz = BWrf/(γ̄Gs) and z0 = f0/(γ̄Gs). In the readout the band is set by the receiver: sampling N points at a dwell time 1/BW resolves frequencies from −BW/2 to +BW/2 in N bins of BW/N, so FOV = BW/(γ̄Gr) and Δx = FOV/N. Fat precesses at Δffat ≈ −3.4 ppm relative to water; the reconstruction maps every frequency as if it were water, so fat is displaced by Δffat/(BW/N) voxels, or Δffat/(γ̄Gr) in millimetres.

Simplifications: B₀ = 3 T. Fat is a single peak at −3.4 ppm (the main methylene peak; real fat has several). The slice profile is the small-tip approximation, i.e. the magnitude of the Fourier transform of a Hann-windowed sinc pulse with time–bandwidth product 4; Δz is its full width at half maximum. The fat slice, which is displaced through the slice in the same way, is not shown. The image is a two-dimensional analytic phantom (ellipses of water and fat) sampled exactly in k-space, with the fat signal given the phase e−i2πΔf·t at each sample time, and reconstructed by an inverse discrete Fourier transform without filtering, so Gibbs ringing is real. The readout has no oversampling or anti-alias filter, so tissue outside the FOV wraps in both directions (scanners usually oversample the readout to avoid this). The phase direction uses the same FOV and matrix as the readout. No relaxation, T2* blurring, coil sensitivity or B₀ inhomogeneity. Noise is white, with a level set by the relative SNR ∝ Δx·Δy·Δz·√(Ny·N/BW) and a fixed random pattern, so that only its amplitude changes as you move the sliders. Signal values: water 0.5 (kidneys 0.85, aorta 0.9), fat 1.0, vertebral marrow fat 0.6 with water 0.15.
