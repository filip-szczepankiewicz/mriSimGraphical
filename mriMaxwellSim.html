What to look for

The concomitant field is quadratic in both position and gradient. A spin echo cancels it only if the waveform is the same on both sides of the 180°, so an asymmetric waveform leaves a residual phase φ(r) = (γm/2B₀)·Q(r). Its gradient along the slice normal dephases spins within each voxel (through-slice signal loss). Its in-plane gradient shifts the echo in k-space, locally and by a different amount across the image, which removes signal and changes the point-spread function where the echo leaves the sampled window — with partial Fourier, much sooner on one side than the other. The constant part does nothing to the magnitude.

Maxwell sim

A teaching aid, not a quantitative simulator. It shows how the concomitant (Maxwell) phase left over after a spin-echo diffusion encoding with an asymmetric waveform corrupts a 2D image placed anywhere in the magnet. The effects that are hard to describe in words — the in-plane echo shifts, their interaction with partial Fourier, and the spatial pattern of the through-slice loss — come out of the simulation rather than being modelled separately.

Concomitant field (lowest order, cylindrically symmetric coil set):

Bc(r,t) = [ (Gxz − ½Gzx)² + (Gyz − ½Gzy)² ] / 2B₀

Residual phase at the echo, for G(t) = g(t)·n with h(t) = +1 before and −1 after the 180°:

φ(r) = (γm / 2B₀) · [ (nxz − ½nzx)² + (nyz − ½nzy)² ],   m = ∫ h(t) g(t)² dt

For a general waveform m generalises to the matrix M = ∫ h(t) G(t)G(t)ᵀ dt, and |m| here equals the Maxwell index √tr(M²) for a single direction. Within the slice the phase is quadratic in the through-slice coordinate s, φ = φ₀ + b·s + a·s², so the slice-averaged signal is ρ·eiφ₀·F(b) with F(b) = (1/Δ)∫ ei(as² + bs) ds over a rectangular profile of thickness Δ. The complex image is built on a 512 × 512 grid, Fourier transformed, cropped to the acquisition matrix (with partial Fourier by zero filling) and transformed back.

Simplifications. A 2D modified Shepp–Logan phantom, extruded through the slice. Ideal rectangular slice profile and ideal refocusing. Only the diffusion encoding gradients contribute; the concomitant phase of imaging gradients, the EPI readout itself, T2/T2* decay, distortion, diffusion attenuation, noise, higher-order concomitant terms (∝ 1/B₀²) and eddy currents are not simulated. The sign convention decides which side of the image partial Fourier hurts; a scanner with the opposite convention mirrors that asymmetry.
