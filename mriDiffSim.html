    Diffusion sim

    The voxel holds a distribution of diffusion tensors D, each a separate Gaussian (free-diffusion) compartment with no exchange between them. The encoding is a b-tensor B = b·B̂. The normalised signal is the average over the distribution, S/S0 = ⟨exp(−B:D)⟩, the standard multidimensional-diffusion signal model.

    Simplifications: no relaxation weighting, no exchange, no time dependence or restriction (each compartment is Gaussian at all b), no noise and no Rician floor. Every tensor is axisymmetric. The b-tensor is drawn as a superquadric whose edges sharpen with its triaxiality τ = 4bΔ²bη² (an ellipsoid when axisymmetric, close to a box at τ = 1); the diffusion tensors are axisymmetric and therefore drawn as ellipsoids. The distribution is represented by 600 tensors drawn with a fixed seed; the voxel shows a representative subset of 64. The powder average uses 144 quasi-uniform b-tensor orientations, so a small orientation ripple can remain for very anisotropic encodings at high b. µFA is computed from the distribution as √(3/2 · ⟨Vλ⟩/(⟨Vλ⟩ + ⟨Diso²⟩)), where Vλ is each tensor's eigenvalue variance.
