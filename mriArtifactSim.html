Distortion countermeasures

Excitation bandwidth

VAT plays the slice gradient again during readout. Off-resonant spins were excited at a shifted slice position, so the extra gradient exactly cancels their readout shift. The price is a blur of about BWRF/BWpx pixels, from weighting the readout by sinc(BWRF·τ), so VAT needs a high readout bandwidth.

EPI acceleration R

Idealised parallel imaging: only the echo train shortens (ESP/R per k-space line) and SNR drops by √R. No g-factor or unfolding errors.

Artifact sim

A teaching aid, not a quantitative simulator. The object is an analytic 2D phantom whose Fourier transform is known exactly, with water and fat as two species. It is sampled on the acquired k-space grid, each sample carrying the time since excitation at which it was measured, and reconstructed by an inverse DFT. The artifacts are therefore not drawn: they follow from sampling, truncation and chemical-shift phase. Simplifications in this version: proton density only (no T1, T2 or T2* weighting, so no T2* blurring along the EPI echo train); a perfectly homogeneous B₀; ideal gradients, with a fixed 0.12 ms ramp overhead per EPI line; a symmetric echo; a single 2D slice with no through-plane effects; white Gaussian noise; fat saturation modelled as uniform 97 % suppression. Susceptibility sources are spheres centred in the slice, with a field that is linear in Δχ (ferromagnetic behaviour is ignored). Through-plane effects of the field (slice bending) are not modelled. Dephasing within a voxel is sampled with up to 4 × 4 points per pixel, which underestimates it where the field changes by more than a cycle between points. View-angle tilting assumes a rectangular slice profile. EPI acceleration is idealised: it shortens the echo train, with no g-factor. Eddy currents are linear fields, one exponential per axis, with no cross-terms or higher spatial orders and no B₀ term apart from the slice offset. Diffusion lobes drive eddy currents but do not attenuate the signal. The Nyquist correction uses the ideal odd–even difference at the centre of the echo train. Readout aliasing, shown when readout oversampling is off, is normally prevented on a scanner by oversampling and the receiver’s low-pass filter.
