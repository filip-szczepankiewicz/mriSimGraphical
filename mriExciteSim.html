Excitation sim

RF excitation of one isochromat by a B₁ field perpendicular to B₀, shown in the lab frame and in a frame rotating at the RF frequency. The B₁ field can be circularly polarised (quadrature drive), linearly polarised (one channel), or circular but counter-rotating (turning against the spins), with a sine or a square-wave carrier, and played as a continuous wave or as a rectangular, sinc or Gaussian pulse. A linear field is the sum of a co-rotating and a counter-rotating circular field of half its amplitude; only the co-rotating one is resonant.

Physics. The Bloch equation is integrated numerically in the lab frame with the full field, so the counter-rotating component is not dropped (no rotating-wave approximation): its 2ω wobble and the Bloch–Siegert shift are real, just exaggerated by the large ω₁/ω₀ used here. The rotating-frame view is the same trajectory, rotated back at ωRF. No relaxation: excitation is assumed much shorter than T1 and T2.

Simplifications. B₁ is drawn hugely enlarged. In a scanner B₁ is about 10 µT against a B₀ of 1.5–3 T, so ω₁/ω₀ is of order 10⁻⁶ and a 90° pulse spans some 10⁵ Larmor periods rather than the handful shown. The travelling-wave panel is an analogy: the RF coil works in the near field, and at 64–128 MHz the wavelength in tissue is comparable to the body. The panel lays out the field the spin experiences over time along a line, exactly as a plane wave travelling along B₀ would carry it. Precession is drawn anticlockwise seen from +z, as in the other simulators; for protons the true sense is clockwise. Mirroring it changes nothing here: "co-rotating" always means turning with the spins.
