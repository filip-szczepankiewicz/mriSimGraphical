Echo train sim

A teaching aid, not a quantitative simulator. In a fast (turbo) spin echo each excitation is followed by a train of refocused echoes, and each echo fills one phase-encoding line. Echo n is weighted by e−n·ESP/T2, so the order in which lines are filled becomes a filter along kPE: the weight at the centre sets the contrast (the effective TE), and its shape across kPE sets the blur and any ringing or ghosts. The object is an analytic head phantom (Shepp–Logan geometry with non-overlapping compartments) whose Fourier transform is exact, so the only blur besides T2 decay is the truncation of k-space at ±N/2, which is also present in the comparison image. Simplifications: full T1 recovery between shots (TR ≫ T1, no T1 weighting); ideal 180° refocusing (no stimulated echoes, no B₁ errors); echo n at exactly n·ESP; no decay during the readout of one line; no noise; no partial Fourier; no magnetisation transfer from the refocusing pulses; a single 2D slice. J-coupling in fat, when switched on, is a phenomenological apparent T2 that lengthens as the echo spacing shortens (after Henkelman et al., JMRI 1992). Tissue values are illustrative.
